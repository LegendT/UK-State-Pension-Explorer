# UK State Pension Explorer — proposed site scope

Prepared 29 September 2026. Revised 29 September 2026 following a precision, consistency and accessibility review. Working title; no domain has been selected. This is a specification, not a statement that the unbuilt site or its content has passed verification.

## 1. Purpose and editorial position

Build a sister site to UK Public Finances Explorer and UK Migration Explorer that helps a reader understand the State Pension, the triple lock, pension adequacy and the consequences of possible reforms.

The primary audience is someone who has heard these subjects discussed in the news and wants an explanation without having to know pensions terminology. The site must also give a careful reader enough evidence to check its claims and reproduce its calculations.

The launch product is a neutral explainer. It presents the strongest substantiated arguments for retaining, changing or replacing the triple lock, and for and against means-testing pension entitlement. It does not engineer its examples to produce a preferred conclusion. Neutrality does not require treating unsupported claims as equally credible.

The owner's concern that removing the triple lock could cause substantial harm is a question to investigate, not a conclusion to encode into the content or calculators.

An optional, separately labelled editorial page can be added later. It must identify its author, distinguish evidence from value judgements, disclose assumptions and link back to the same evidence as the explainer.

**Primary visitor outcome:** the reader can explain what is being proposed, distinguish the policy choices, understand who might gain or lose, and identify which conclusions depend on assumptions.

**Non-negotiable requirements:** WCAG 2.2 Level AA conformance; traceable evidence; reproducible arithmetic; visible uncertainty; a correction process; and a homepage that begins with the current pension amount, accurately qualified.

## 2. Launch structure: eight pages

### A. Overview — Start here

Lead with the amount, before an introductory essay, political context or navigation to a calculator. The opening content is specified as:

> **How much is the State Pension?**
>
> **£241.30 a week**
>
> Full new State Pension, 2026/27, before tax.
>
> £12,547.60 over 52 weeks, calculated as £241.30 × 52.
>
> **Full basic State Pension: £184.90 a week.**
>
> These are full rates, not everyone's payment. Your amount depends on your pension record and any additional entitlement; some people receive less and some receive more.

The two weekly amounts were checked on 29 September 2026 against [GOV.UK: new State Pension](https://www.gov.uk/new-state-pension/what-youll-get) and [GOV.UK: basic State Pension](https://www.gov.uk/state-pension/how-much-you-get). Recheck the rates, period and effective dates before publication. Treat this block as dated source-backed content, not permanent text to copy separately into each page. The annual figure is a 52-week equivalent, not a promise of an individual's exact tax-year receipts.

Keep both rate labels, the individual-entitlement qualification, source links and checking date visible without opening an accordion. Do not call either figure “the maximum pension”. Link directly to “Which pension applies?” and the official forecast service. Forecast or announced future rates must be separate from the rate currently payable.

Then explain briefly what the State Pension is, what the triple lock does, and why entitlement and uprating are different questions.

Include:

- A concise introduction and links to the three main journeys: understand the rules, understand buying power, explore reform.
- The opening rate block above, generated from the same records used by the rest of the site.
- A dated summary of the news context, separating confirmed policy, proposals, reported possibilities and commentary.
- A small number of essential distinctions, each linking to the relevant explanation.
- Sources and verification dates beside headline facts.

Avoid making a political news story the permanent organising principle of the site. The page should remain useful when the current debate moves on.

### B. The State Pension — What people receive and why

Explain:

- The purpose of the State Pension and its place alongside workplace and personal pensions.
- The basic and new systems, including why their headline rates cannot be treated as complete, directly comparable descriptions of every recipient's income.
- National Insurance contributions and credits; qualifying years; transitional rules and contracting out. Avoid an unconditional claim that a fixed number of years guarantees every person the full pension.
- State Pension age, claiming and deferral, with links to the official services for personal circumstances.
- Additional and protected payments, and why not every component follows the same uprating rule.
- Tax treatment, payment frequency and the difference between a four-week payment and a calendar-month equivalent.
- Pension Credit and relevant help with housing, Council Tax and disability costs, clearly separated from State Pension entitlement.
- How the pension is financed, including the distinction between a contributory entitlement and a personal invested pension pot.
- Overseas uprating as a clearly signposted exception requiring its own official source.

Use expandable detail so that technical exceptions remain available without overwhelming the opening explanation. This page is not a personal entitlement calculator.

### C. The triple lock — How annual increases work

Explain the three measures, their reference periods and the timing of the annual increase. Distinguish the triple-lock commitment from the underlying legal uprating requirements.

Include the mechanism demonstrator described below, a worked example using verified historical inputs, the policy's history and exceptions, and an explanation of compounding: one year's increase changes the starting amount for later increases.

Explain what the rule protects against and its limits: a national inflation measure is not each person's bill, and an annual adjustment cannot track every price change as it occurs.

### D. Living on a pension — What the money can cover

Separate three different questions:

1. What do pensioner households actually spend, according to surveys?
2. What does research estimate a minimum acceptable living standard would cost?
3. What does a particular household need, given its own circumstances?

Use clearly dated research budgets and the household budget explorer. Explain housing costs, shared household costs, regional differences, transport access, energy needs, disability and care expenses. Include occasional expenditure such as appliance replacement, rather than showing only regular bills.

Show State Pension income separately from other pensions, earnings, savings income, tax and benefit support. Never imply a household must meet all its costs from the State Pension alone, or assume that additional support is received automatically.

Explain inflation rates versus accumulated price increases. Compare pension growth and prices over aligned periods; do not treat one current inflation reading as a complete test of pension adequacy.

Make three additional distinctions explicit: a research benchmark is not a statutory entitlement; measured poverty and falling below a budget standard are different outcomes; and a low recorded expenditure may reflect going without rather than low need. Changes in a benchmark can reflect changed assumptions or a changed basket as well as prices.

For observed spending, report survey coverage, sample limitations, dates and the precise household grouping. Do not turn a household survey average into a claim about how an individual spends their State Pension. Where comparable breakdowns are unavailable, say so.

Specify examples covering single and couple households, owners without a mortgage, social renters and private renters. State locality and price date. If a renter's budget is adapted for an owner, check repairs, insurance and other ownership costs rather than merely deleting rent. Explain that annualised replacement allowances smooth irregular costs and do not establish that a household has cash available for an emergency. State unsupported cases such as mixed-age or multi-adult households at the tool entry point, with appropriate guidance links.

### E. Reform options — What changes, who gains and who loses?

Give substantial, separately labelled treatment to two policy decisions:

**Changing annual increases:** retain the triple lock; remove the minimum floor while keeping prices and earnings protection; link to earnings; link to prices; or explain a sourced longer-term earnings rule with temporary inflation protection. Do not include a pension freeze among the launch comparison choices without a specific relevant proposal: it risks making ordinary uprating reform appear equivalent to freezing payments. The more complex earnings rule remains explanatory content until its model is fully specified and verified.

**Changing entitlement:** means-test income; assess assets as well as income; retain the pension and redistribute through taxation; or retain broad entitlement while strengthening targeted support. Explain that an assessment of resources is different from an assessment of individual needs.

For each option examine:

- Effects on income, purchasing power and living standards relative to workers.
- Effects on people with low income, modest private pensions, high income and valuable but illiquid assets.
- Current pensioners, people approaching retirement and future pensioners.
- Public spending, tax receipts, benefit interactions, administration and behavioural uncertainty.
- Saving incentives, take-up, threshold effects, transition arrangements and confidence in retirement planning.
- What the evidence can establish and what remains a judgement about fairness.

Use the policy comparison tool for uprating. Means-testing gets an evidence-led explanation at launch, not an invented national savings calculator. A means-testing simulation requires a defined policy and suitable distributional data before it can be scoped credibly.

**Required public-finance context:** show annual State Pension expenditure, recipients and a consistent GDP comparison where evidenced. Identify financial year, coverage, outturn versus forecast, and included pension components; do not treat State Pension expenditure as all spending on older people. Separate the full pension bill, the estimated cost attributable to a particular uprating baseline, and prospective savings from a specified reform. Explain the roles of demographics, pension age, entitlement and uprating rather than attributing all future expenditure growth to the triple lock. When quoting fiscal savings, specify annual versus cumulative, cash versus constant prices, gross versus net, start date and whether existing entitlements are retained.

**Required distributional context:** examine the distribution of pensioner household incomes and resources, not just an average pensioner. Where reliable data permit, distinguish income from wealth, individuals from households, and before-housing-cost from after-housing-cost measures. Identify how disability, caring history, household composition and unequal private pension provision affect exposure to reform. Do not substitute illustrative personas for population estimates or infer that the top of an income distribution has no needs. Use “low/high income” only with a definition. Report poverty measures with the publisher's definitions, uncertainty and survey coverage.

Use the same comparison questions, source standard and presentation space for retaining and replacing the existing rule. Potential harms of change and costs of retaining the current policy both belong in the analysis; “balanced” does not mean equal confidence in unequal evidence.

### F. Pensions and social care — Assessing the proposed trade-off

Explain what a pension payment provides and what social-care services provide. Establish the geographic scope of each proposal: UK pension policy and a care proposal for one nation cannot silently be treated as covering the same population.

Separate current arrangements, announced commitments, costed proposals and media speculation. Date each policy-status statement.

Assess a proposal, when specified, against consistent questions:

- What replacement pension rule is proposed, from when and for whom?
- What care would be covered, for which people, with which eligibility conditions?
- Who loses income, who receives services, and when?
- What are the gross and net fiscal effects, measured against which baseline?
- Would savings actually be allocated to care, or is that an illustrative possibility?
- What transitional funding and delivery capacity would be required?

Do not equate the accumulated cost of past triple-lock increases with immediately available savings from changing future increases. Do not suggest every person giving up pension income receives an equivalent value of care.

### G. History — What has changed over time?

Provide an accessible chart and data table of actual pension rates and uprating decisions, with relevant policy events.

Offer nominal amounts, inflation-adjusted amounts and a clearly defined earnings comparison where compatible data exist. Keep basic and new State Pension series distinct; do not join them into an apparent single person's pension history.

A counterfactual comparison can show what alternative annual rules would have produced from a common starting point. Label it as a calculation, specify the start date and measures, and account for historical exceptions. Explain that this is not an estimate of what every person would actually have received under a different pension system.

### H. Sources, method and corrections

Describe the source hierarchy, calculation methods, model limits, dates, editorial approach and correction process. Include a concise glossary, ownership information, disclosure of AI assistance and the actual review process used.

Give readers source links beside claims as well as a central register. Publish substantive corrections with dates and explanations. Provide an agreed, working route to report errors; do not invent an email address or copy a sibling site's contact details without checking.

Keep essential definitions beside the first use as well as in the glossary. Include common misunderstandings within the relevant pages rather than adding a separate claims section at launch. Link to accessible utility pages for the accessibility statement and privacy information; “eight pages” refers to the eight main subject pages, not a cap that excludes these necessities.

## 3. Launch tools

### Tool 1: How the triple lock works

**Inputs:** inflation, earnings growth, starting weekly amount of the pension component being modelled, and a choice of a clearly labelled historical example or hypothetical values. Default to a sourced full-rate example. Do not invite readers to enter their entire pension payment as if every component necessarily has the same annual increase.

**Outputs:** the selected uprating percentage, the determining measure or tie, weekly increase, new weekly amount and annual equivalent.

**Requirements:** explain reference-period lags; show the formula; state what pension component is modelled; validate inputs. Historical examples must match the actual policy applied that year rather than mechanically imposing the current rule on every year.

Keep historical examples and hypothetical settings visibly distinct, including after a reader changes an input. Use the actual published uprating decision for observed history; an arithmetic maximum from a later-revised statistical series is not a substitute for what was paid.

### Tool 2: Compare the replacement rules

**Inputs:** a common starting pension, reform start, duration, policy rule and inflation/earnings path. Offer a small set of declared scenarios, including steady growth, weak earnings and volatile prices, alongside editable assumptions.

**Outputs:** pension amounts in cash, purchasing power and relative to a defined earnings baseline; the annual difference from retaining the triple lock; and cumulative pension received over the selected fixed period. Do not call that total a lifetime loss. State whether cumulative amounts are nominal or expressed in constant prices; do not add unlike price bases.

**Requirements:** all rules use the same economic path for a given comparison. Include cases where rules give the same result. State rounding, deflation and payment-period conventions. Distinguish a lower payment than a counterfactual from an actual fall in pounds. Display a data table and plain-English result alongside the chart.

Model the relevant pension component before tax. Label excluded effects, including tax and means-tested benefits. Do not scale an individual's difference into national savings by multiplying by an undifferentiated pensioner count.

Launch with four completely specified rules: triple lock, prices-or-earnings double lock, earnings link and prices link. No preferred policy is preselected as a recommendation. A scenario is not a probability forecast. Give a named reason for each illustrative path and allow all comparisons to be reset to the same assumptions.

Before implementation, write the yearly timing convention, start-of-period amount, earnings definition, inflation index/base date, treatment of negative growth, rounding order and all floors. If an alternative assumes payments cannot fall in cash terms, label that explicitly as part of that hypothetical rule, not as a claim about enacted law. Explain that smoothing a rule can affect the result and specify any catch-up mechanism before modelling it. Either apply observed publication lags consistently or state that a hypothetical model simplifies them.

Use an earnings index comparison unless a compatible absolute earnings denominator has been verified. Forecasts require a specified edition and horizon; beyond that horizon use explicitly separate assumptions. Omit probability bands unless supported by an actual statistical model.

### Tool 3: What does the pension cover?

**Inputs:** single or two-person household; gross pension and other income; benefit support actually received; household income tax as a separate user-entered amount; housing costs; editable spending categories. Label the period of every input and offer sourced example budgets with their price dates and geographic assumptions.

**Outputs:** income less tax, spending and remaining amount or shortfall, weekly and annually, with spending divided across categories. If tax is unknown, explicitly show a before-tax comparison and do not label the balance spendable income. The launch tool is a current-budget snapshot, not a projection of future household disposable income.

**Requirements:** this is a budget explorer, not an entitlement determination. Presets must distinguish researched needs from observed spending. Housing and additional needs must be visible, not buried in notes. Do not infer that a benchmark covers disability or care costs. Show a shortfall without forcing expenditure to fit available income.

Do not calculate benefits or tax from incomplete household information. Use one clear accounting convention: enter cash benefits once as income and gross bills once as expenditure; reflect bill discounts in the relevant bill, not again as income. Do not duplicate tax deducted from an income field in the tax line. State preset tax assumptions beside results. Link to authoritative personal checks and explain benefit non-take-up.

Do not automatically feed the before-tax policy comparison into a future budget. That would require specified future costs, tax, benefit interactions and other income. This connection is deferred. Provide category-specific price-date information; any inflation update to a published basket is the site's labelled estimate, distinct from the source's published budget.

### Tool 4: Pension history explorer

**Inputs:** pension series, date range and available comparison measure.

**Outputs:** actual rates and decisions, a selectable calculation under alternative rules where evidence permits, chart annotations and a downloadable or copyable underlying table.

**Requirements:** keep this tool historical at launch; future scenarios belong in Tool 2. Mark observed data and reconstructed comparisons distinctly. Tool 4 shares relevant calculation definitions with Tool 2, but observed uprating decisions remain separate inputs, including exceptional years. Do not backdate the new pension series into years before that system existed. The starting pension and starting year of every counterfactual must be visible, because changing either changes the result.

## 4. Evidence and trust requirements

Every material numerical claim needs a central record containing:

- Identifier, definition, value, unit and population covered.
- Individual or household basis; geographic coverage; reference period and price basis.
- Gross/net and weekly/monthly/annual conventions where relevant.
- Publisher, exact publication title, source URL, publication date and retrieval/checking date.
- Specific table, series or passage supporting the value.
- Classification: published rate/rule, observed statistic, estimate, forecast, derived calculation or hypothetical scenario; provisional status recorded separately.
- Qualifications, known revisions and the source of any disagreement.
- Formula and input records for calculated values.
- Effective-from and effective-to dates for rules/rates, separate from publication and retrieval dates; the time basis and release vintage of every model input.
- Review status, reviewer/date, next review trigger and a retained lawful evidence extract or source snapshot sufficient to investigate subsequent revisions.

Store a factual value once and reuse it in prose, tables and charts. A change to a rate must not leave an old value in a calculator or headline.

Use dated language that says what was actually checked. Do not display “verified”, “official” or “independently reviewed” as a general badge of reliability. Describe the check and its limitations.

Review prose as well as arithmetic. Correct numbers can still support a misleading sentence, an invalid comparison or an unjustified causal claim.

Do not silently resolve conflicting sources. Identify differences in period, scope, definitions, revisions or methods; omit an unresolved headline comparison rather than manufacture agreement.

Qualitative claims need evidence too. Maintain a claim register linking each material statement to its source and supporting passage, with an explicit flag for interpretation or editorial judgement. Distinguish “the source says this” from “the evidence establishes this”; official guidance and research summaries can themselves require qualification. Separate statistical revisions, policy changes and errors made by this project in the correction log.

Record actual checks rather than refreshing every page's date when one figure changes. Site-wide currentness cannot be inferred from the newest source. Citation displays must remain understandable to a non-specialist; expose technical details on demand without hiding a qualification essential to interpreting the number.

Research starting points already identified in this discussion include the sibling sites' methods, GOV.UK pension guidance, Commons Library pensions briefings, IFS pension reviews, ONS inflation publications and the September 2026 Minimum Income Standard report. Inclusion here is not certification that any figure is ready to publish.

## 6. Design and accessibility

**Required standard: WCAG 2.2 Level AA.** Conformance covers all applicable Level A and AA success criteria, complete pages and complete processes, including all four tools and their error/result states. This is a release requirement, not an aspiration or an automated-score target. Assess against [the W3C standard](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); a short checklist does not replace it.

Cover text and non-text contrast, text resizing and spacing, reflow, semantic structure, keyboard operation, visible and unobscured focus, labels and errors, status messages, non-drag alternatives and minimum target sizing. Record criterion-specific exceptions only where the standard actually permits them. Set a larger 44-by-44 CSS-pixel target as a design preference for primary controls, distinct from the AA minimum requirements.

Match the siblings' plain-English, restrained presentation, visible sourcing and clear numerical definitions. Use progressive detail: a direct answer first, explanation next, supporting evidence and technical notes close by.

Use consistent treatment of dates, units, policy status and calculated values. Avoid emotionally loaded photographs, political imagery or colours that imply a preferred policy.

Charts must have readable axes, labels, units, accessible descriptions and equivalent tables. Never rely on colour alone. Inputs must work by keyboard and touch, with number fields alongside sliders where useful. Results and errors need to be understandable to assistive technology.

Maintain a criterion-by-criterion assessment with pass, fail or justified not-applicable results and reproducible evidence. Combine automated audits with manual keyboard, zoom/reflow, text-spacing, touch and screen-reader checks. W3C states that tools alone cannot determine conformance; see [its evaluation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/).

Test every main page and unique component, plus representative normal, error, expanded-detail and updated-result states in each tool. Record browser, operating-system and assistive-technology versions actually used. The intended manual matrix includes VoiceOver with Safari and NVDA with a supported Windows browser; if an environment or qualified evaluation is unavailable, record the gap and arrange it before claiming that coverage. Do not invent an independent audit or screen-reader-user test. Include readers with disabilities and older readers in usability evaluation where feasible, without treating usability sessions as a substitute for conformance assessment.

No known unresolved A/AA failure can be described away as a caveat while claiming full conformance. Publish an accessibility statement giving scope, assessment date, method and a working route to report barriers. If assessment is incomplete, retain an explicitly unverified preview; the public launch requirement remains unchanged.

Process budget inputs locally by default, without storing or transmitting personal amounts. No account is needed. Essential explanations should remain readable if interactive features fail.

Keep entered amounts out of URLs, analytics and diagnostic reports. Explain whether reload clears inputs. Provide accessible page anchors and a short introductory summary on each substantial page, so depth does not require reading a long essay to find the answer. Do not hide essential rate qualifications or scenario assumptions solely in tooltips.

## 7. Delivery stages and launch acceptance

### Stage 1: Evidence and content foundation

Produce the detailed page outline, numerical and qualitative claim registers, glossary, data definitions, model specification and clearly labelled household examples. Resolve source mismatches and write the neutral argument summaries before polishing visual design. Define the accessibility assessment matrix and the ongoing editorial responsibilities at this stage.

### Stage 2: Complete first version

Build the eight pages and four bounded tools. Integrate citations and assumptions as part of the interface. Write all empty, invalid-input and unavailable-data states. Show missing evidence as a limitation rather than filling it with an invented value.

### Stage 3: Verification and publication preparation

Review every material factual claim against its cited source. Reproduce representative calculator results independently, including ties, rounding, zero and negative input paths where supported, and years affected by exceptional rules. Reconcile displayed totals and grouped budget categories.

Use worked examples calculated separately from the implementation, not tests that merely repeat its logic. Check period conversions, price bases, component coverage, cumulative totals, and sensitivity to starting year and economic assumptions. Reject any result where a difference before tax is described as a change in take-home income. Review chart prose and alternate tables against the same underlying records.

Review all policy options for asymmetric assumptions, loaded wording and unsupported implications. Confirm that savings baselines, price dates and UK/nation scope are visible at the point of use.

Complete the WCAG 2.2 AA assessment, responsive checks, link checks and source-freshness review. Record what was actually checked. Assign a content owner and publication reviewer; an author rereading their own work is not independent review. A second review of headline claims, model definitions and policy comparisons is recommended, and its actual status must be disclosed rather than assumed. Public release should follow the agreed editorial review; this scope does not itself publish a site.

**Launch is ready when:**

- A first-time reader can distinguish pension entitlement, annual uprating and means-tested support.
- All material claims have traceable evidence and appropriate qualifications.
- Every calculator is reproducible and labels its assumptions and omissions.
- Household examples cannot be mistaken for a universal pensioner budget or a benefits award.
- The social-care page accurately separates enacted policy from proposals and reporting.
- Readers can use the site on a phone, with a keyboard and with accessible chart alternatives.
- All applicable WCAG 2.2 A and AA criteria have a documented assessment, with no unresolved failures; the public accessibility statement accurately reflects that assessment.
- Headline rates have been rechecked for their effective period, and are not presented as universal or maximum payments.
- National spending, household distribution and model outputs use clearly identified populations, baselines, units and dates.
- A real correction route and a dated source register are available.

## 8. Maintenance and later additions

Review rates and benefit rules when official changes are published, model inputs when their source releases change, forecasts when superseded and the news panel when policy status changes. Prioritise corrections that change a reader's conclusion. Do not promise live data or unattended publication.

Before launch, assign each source an owner and a review trigger/deadline. Required triggers include rate-effective dates, relevant price/earnings releases, new budget research, replacement fiscal forecasts and confirmed changes in policy status. A news note without a maintained review process should be a dated historical note, not a “latest news” module. If a current claim becomes unsupported or is superseded, remove its current-status label and show a dated notice or withdraw the affected result pending correction. Retest changed components for accessibility and impacted calculations when content, dependencies or design changes.

No recurring automation is included in this scope. A later source-monitoring process could flag changes for review without automatically publishing revised claims.

Defer the separate editorial page, international comparisons, comprehensive personal entitlement calculations, tax/benefit optimisation, national means-testing cost models, extensive regional datasets and a full social-care funding simulator. Each needs additional evidence and specification; none is required to explain the core subject accurately.

The working name, domain and hosting remain implementation decisions. Correction contact, content ownership, review capacity and the accessibility evaluation arrangement are launch dependencies. They do not prevent evidence gathering and content preparation. Do not add pages merely to accommodate every topic: the eight-page structure groups related questions, while the restricted model scope keeps the first release auditable.
