## Method and its limits

This is an expert review: a heuristic evaluation by an AI evaluator, against published guidance. It is not an observation of use. Four passes were run, each by a separate agent with one lens and no sight of the others:

1. Findability and organisation.
2. Length, layout, density and repetition.
3. Comprehension, sources and trust.
4. Charts, tables and tools.

The dashboard pass was not run, because no page is a dashboard. The findings were merged by root cause. Severity was rated on the 0 to 4 scale below by four raters, the four pass agents, each without sight of the others, and the mean was used, a half rounded up. The raters are the same agents that found the problems, as the skill's method sets out, so they are independent of each other but not of the findings, which Nielsen's method would prefer. Frequency is an estimate.

User evidence available: none. The site is not published, so there is no analytics, site search or feedback, and no user test has been run. The owner's page review of 8 October 2026 (notes 1 to 13) is an expert reading, not user evidence. The claims here therefore cover structure, layout and wording only, and a change is not shown to help readers until it is tested.

Built from branch `launch-review` at commit `db88af4`, served in development mode, measured with Chrome 154 at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1280 px with the `statistics-ux` scripts. First-screen pictures of all 19 pages at 320, 390 and 1280. Section pictures at 390 and 1280 of every section holding a table, chart or tool and every section over three phone screens (28 sections). The four tools driven one control at a time at 390, and the two chart tools again at 320 and 1280. The project's accessibility checks were read report-only on the same build: no axe violations, no sideways scroll.

Not done:

- content accuracy, beyond presentation against each page's own source
- dark mode
- keyboard and screen readers, which are launch step 4
- combinations of tool controls beyond those the passes tried by hand
- items the passes marked Not checked: ranges on the overview and living (item 19), a production build, which would remove the preview banner (item 25), the contrast of chart marks against each other, print, looked at in Chrome only, and find-in-page in no browser, and the register rows for item 23

Two defects found during the review were fixed at once, outside the review's own scope, which was to change no page: the assistant judged them defects in work already approved, W1 to W3. They are not findings here. The budget explorer's method said "The tool never changes an amount you entered", which W3 made false. Five pages kept a "last changed" date of 5 October 2026 after their content changed on 8 October: reform options, costs, social care, the budget explorer, and sources and method. Commit `f0967a7`. Two more pages that pass 3 marked for item 24, the overview and the site map, were not changed: see smaller points. The owner can ask for the fix to be reversed and handled as a recommendation.

Severity, on Nielsen's scale (principle M5), rated from frequency, impact and persistence:

- 0: not a usability problem at all
- 1: cosmetic problem only
- 2: minor usability problem, low priority
- 3: major usability problem, high priority
- 4: usability catastrophe, imperative to fix

## Findings across the site

### F1. The open source block under a paragraph weighs as much as the paragraph and attaches to the next one (severity 3)

At 390 px a source block is 155 to 266 px tall, against a paragraph mean of 213 px on costs and 160 px on the State Pension page. It has 16 px above it and 0 px below wherever the next element is a paragraph, so only the summary's 9.6 px padding separates it from the next paragraph: closer to the next paragraph than to its own in 9 of 15 blocks on costs and 3 of 10 on the State Pension page. It opens with a bordered "Estimate" or "Observed statistic" pill, which reads as the start of a new item. At 1280 px a block is taller than its paragraph in 12 of 15 cases on costs. Blocks take 27.9%, 23.3% and 19.5% of `main` on costs, the State Pension page and living. Identical blocks sit next to each other on three pages. The owner's note 9, points (c) and (d), holds. (d), figures restated under a table, holds on costs only. S6, S10, S15.

What works, to keep: the kind of figure and its key caveat stay open (decision 49), with "More about this figure" as the second layer and the print rule. The single muted line of decision 77 already reads well: entitlement has 21 such lines and no blocks, triple lock 19 and none. Costs shows both patterns on one screen, and the line is the clearer.

### F2. On a phone both tool charts open at their left end, with no cue, in 8 to 8.7 px text (severity 3)

On the rule comparison and the history explorer the chart is a fixed 480 px wide. At 320 px, 220 px of it shows, about 4 of 10 years, and at 390 px, 290 px. It opens at the left, `scrollLeft` 0, with nothing to say it scrolls, so the end of the lines, where the title's finding is, is off screen. With "Triple lock, applied mechanically" added, a phone reader sees the calculated line below the published one at first. It ends £6.60 above, off screen. Chart text is 8.7 px on the comparison and 8 px on the history explorer, at both widths. Wide tables are in labelled, focusable scroll regions, which works for reflow, but none has a cue either, and the history table shows its least useful columns first. This settles audit finding A11 and sweep item C2. S11, S13, S14.

What works, to keep: the message title in HTML. The open data table straight under each chart. Both charts read well at 1280 px.

### F3. On 12 of 58 figure cards the caveat shown open is not the one that changes the meaning (severity 3)

The card shows the first sentence of its `qualifications` (decision 49). On 12 cards that sentence is a bare kind ("A survey estimate.", "Indicative only."), a note that "The passage runs across a page break in the source" (the 71% card on costs), or nothing (the two IFS 2050 cards). On social care, "Official statistics in development" is the hidden second sentence. Accredited, official or in-development status is shown only under "About the source", or not at all, on 8 pages. The figure register on sources and method shows no caveat. This is the trap recorded on 30 September 2026 ("First sentence as the key caveat") repeated. S10, S16.

What works, to keep: the cards where it is right, among them the wealth card ("accreditation is suspended" first), "A full rate, not everyone's payment." and the HBAI range line.

### F4. Figures in prose sit under another body's citation (severity 3)

A figure placed in prose with `m.f` carries no citation of its own, so the next citation, for another claim, reads as its source. On entitlement: 14%, a DWP figure, under "Age UK (October 2023)". 62% under the Institute for Fiscal Studies. 30.2%, an IFS figure, under the Pensions Commission, which says 30%. 820,000 and £35,000 with no citation. On social care the Health Foundation's and the King's Fund's words sit under "Source: Age UK" (note 10, W10, already replaces that source). A reader quoting the 14% would credit Age UK in 2023. S7, S8.

What works, to keep: every figure comes from the register, and every claim carries its own citation.

### F5. Figures in the arguments lack their range, period or comparison, and two sentences overclaim (severity 3)

On entitlement, 14% and 62% are given without range or period, and "pensioner poverty has risen since 2011-12" without levels, the same kind of omission as note 5. On costs, the IFS "£5 billion to £40 billion" sentence does not say what it is compared with, so note 8 applies on costs too. On the triple lock and reform options pages, "does not fall behind … average pay" and "at least as fast as prices and as earnings, every year" are false on their own for 2022/23, when Parliament set the earnings part aside. S7, S8.

What works, to keep: the cost and saving tables with their "Compared with" column, and "Three different amounts are often confused".
