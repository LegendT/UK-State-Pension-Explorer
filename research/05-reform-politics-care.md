Research file for UK State Pension Explorer. Retrieval date for every record: **2026-09-29** (retrieved between about 09:20 and 10:00 BST).

## How to read this file

**Status categories:** ENACTED / ANNOUNCED COMMITMENT / COSTED PROPOSAL / PROPOSAL / REPORTED / COMMENTARY / RESEARCH FINDING / RESEARCH RECOMMENDATION.

**Check status:**

- **FETCHED-AND-QUOTED** — the source page or PDF was opened and the quote was copied from its text.
- **SECONDARY-ONLY** — the quote or fact comes from a named intermediary (for example a news report of what someone said), not from the speaker's own document.
- **NOT-FOUND** — looked for and not found.

**Method note.** Pages were downloaded and converted to text, and quotes were copied from that text. Sites that blocked automated download (IFS articles, TUC, ITV, Health Foundation, OECD, Services Australia) were opened in a browser and the rendered text was read. Parliament sites (Hansard, Commons Library, committee publications) refused both methods, so nothing in this file is quoted from them.

**Known limits, stated once:**

1. **The Prime Minister's conference speech had not been delivered when this research was done.** Every account of what it contains is advance briefing ("will say", "is expected to"). No transcript was on labour.org.uk or GOV.UK at retrieval. One aggregator page (uk.headtopics.com, summarising the Daily Mail) is written as if the speech had been given; its content could not be verified and it contradicts itself. It is not used as evidence of anything.
2. Quotes from news reports are verbatim from the report. They are evidence that the outlet reported those words, not independent proof the words were spoken.
3. Secondary reports disagree on some ministers' job titles. This file gives a title only where the source quoted gives it.

### A2. What has been proposed or reported in 2026

**Headline finding.** As of the morning of 29 September 2026, **no party, commission or government document was found that proposes replacing or removing the triple lock to fund social care.** What exists is:

- (a) press reports, sourced to advance briefing and unnamed insiders, that the Prime Minister will say the triple lock should be *reviewed* in Labour's *next* manifesto;
- (b) a commentator's inference (Robert Peston) that this is the funding plan, which he says he is not certain of;
- (c) on-the-record remarks by individual Labour figures for and against;
- (d) think-tank proposals to replace the triple lock that are **not** tied to social care.

The care plan concerns **England**. The State Pension is **UK-wide**. A trade-off between them would therefore take money from pensioners in all four nations to fund a service in one, unless devolved funding consequences were arranged. No source found addresses this.

## D. Premises in the brief that were wrong or unsupported

1. **"Replacing the triple lock to fund social care has been proposed."** Not supported. It is reported, inferred and argued over. No party policy, commission recommendation or government document says it.
2. **The Casey Commission has two phases reporting in 2026 and 2028.** Superseded. The PM announced on 29 July 2026 that it will report by summer 2027. No phase 1 report was found.
3. **Wales: "the £50,000 capital limit" alongside the non-residential cap.** £50,000 is the residential limit. The non-residential figure is £24,000.
4. **Attendance Allowance is a GB-wide benefit.** Not since Scotland introduced Pension Age Disability Payment.
5. **England's figures come from an NHS England "Activity and Finance Report".** DHSC now publishes finance and activity separately.
6. **A 2011–2013 smoothed earnings link discussion.** Not found. The precedent found is the Work and Pensions Committee report of 2016, and a Resolution Foundation article of 2020.
7. **Badenoch's means-testing remark.** It was January 2025, and she is reported to have restated support for the triple lock later that year.
8. **The Pensions Commission's remit on the triple lock.** Neither in nor out by name. The TBI and Resolution Foundation both urge it to take a view; the terms of reference do not require it.
