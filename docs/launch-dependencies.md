   Done, 8 October 2026: all 19 pages. The owner finished the review on 8 October 2026. Whether `REVIEW_DATE` is that date, or the date the owner checks the fixed pages, is for the owner to decide before step 7.

   Notes so far:
   - **Note 1, overview:** add the "On this page" list. The page has 10 sections and none, because the UX review shortened it instead (R5). On a phone, place it after "How much is the State Pension?" so the answer stays on the first screen.
   - **Note 2, all long pages:** follow NN/g, "Back-to-Top Button Design Guidelines" (2017). Remove the "Back to contents" link at the end of each section. On pages longer than 4 screens, add one sticky "Back to top" link, with text and an up arrow, in the lower right. It shows when the reader scrolls up, and it does not move after that. Unless the owner says otherwise, it shows only below 1024 px, where the contents list does not stay in view (decision 50). It must sit above the pinned tool summary, never cover a focused element (SC 2.4.11), and work at 400% zoom. Closes the open question of 1 October 2026; becomes decision 88.
   - **Note 3, the State Pension:** the age and date rules read as repetition. Two different April 2016 tests, reaching State Pension age and when the National Insurance record started, are stated about 13 times and blur together. Proposal: one table near the top, "What your date of birth decides", giving the pension, State Pension age and years needed. The record-start rule is stated once, as a separate test. Later sections link back instead of restating. The rate cards keep their one "Who it applies to" line.
   - **Note 4, the reform options section, all 5 pages:** two lists of links sit one after the other and look the same. One lists the pages in the section, the other the sections on this page. Their headings do not make the difference clear. The opening paragraph of reform options lists the pages a third time. Proposal: merge them into one "Reform options" list, with the current page expanded to show its sections. Alternative: keep two, and give the page list a different form, such as GOV.UK's "Part 1 of 5" with Next and Previous links at the foot.
   - **Note 5, entitlement, "Fairness between generations":** claim `rf-living-standards` gives the Resolution Foundation's growth finding without the levels, so it reads as if pensioners are better off. The same report says the growth started from a lower base, and that a typical pensioner household "now has the same level of income as the typical working-age household", level since 2011-12. Add that passage beside the growth claim.
   - **Note 6, entitlement, "An older population costs more than the lock":** "the cost of an older population" reads as the whole cost of ageing. The Pensions Policy Institute's passage is about the increase in State Pension spending only. Reword the heading and claim `ppi-demographics`: more pensioners, not the triple lock, cause most of the rise in State Pension spending. Consider showing the Office for Budget Responsibility comparison that is in the claim's note.
   - **Note 7, entitlement, "For keeping the triple lock":** an argument is missing. A fixed rule takes the increase out of each government's hands, and pensioners cannot adjust to a badly run economy as workers can. The page has the reply, the Resolution Foundation's "rewards volatility", but not the argument it answers. Find a source first, such as Age UK, the Pensions Policy Institute, or the Pensions Commission on the earnings link lost in 1980.
   - **Note 8, entitlement, "Its future cost can only be estimated, with wide uncertainty":** proposed by the assistant. The page does not say that the range of £5 billion to £40 billion in 2050 is measured against an earnings link. So a reader cannot tell that an earnings link removes most of the uncertainty. Add one sentence on what the range compares with, and link to reform options for how each rule changes it. Any claim about the effect of the April 2030 rule needs a source first.
   - **Note 9, all pages, readability:** sections and their contents run together. (1) `h3` is the same size as body text and `h4` is smaller (`main.css` lines 69 and 70), so sub-headings read as bold paragraphs. (2) A major section break is a 24 px heading with about 40 px above it, and nothing else. (3) The source block under a paragraph, with a badge, Period, Place, Source and "More about this figure", is as heavy as the paragraph. It has the same space above and below, so a reader cannot tell which paragraph it belongs to, and the "Estimate" badge reads as a new item. Apply the pattern of decision 77: one muted line close under its paragraph, the rest inside "More about this figure", and a clear gap after. (4) Where a table already lists the estimates, as in "The saving from a change" on the costs page, cut the paragraphs to what the table cannot say. Measured on the costs page at 1280 px and 390 px. The stylesheet and the block template apply to every page.
   - **Note 10, social care:** on 8 October 2026 the owner confirmed both statements on the organisations' own sites, from captures of their press releases of 29 September 2026. They are the Health Foundation (Dr Jennifer Dixon) and the King's Fund (Sarah Woolnough). Add both as source records, and cite them in place of the trade report. Remove the sentence that says their sites refuse automated reading, so the paragraph covers only Unite's statement as reported by PA News. This closes "The Health Foundation and the King's Fund" in "Waiting on the owner".
   - **Note 11, history explorer:** a calculated rule that starts before 2022/23 uses the earnings growth of 8.3% that Parliament set aside that year. So the double lock comes out above the published rate: £248.30 against £241.30 for 2016/17 to 2026/27. That is accurate, but "At a glance" gives no reason, and it reads as if the double lock is more generous than the triple lock. When the range includes 2022/23, add one line to "At a glance": the calculation uses earnings growth of 8.3% for 2022/23, which Parliament set aside that year, and the published rate rose by 3.1%.
   - **Note 12, history explorer:** "Triple lock, applied mechanically" is unclear where the reader chooses it. The double lock option is applied in the same way and does not say so. Rename both to say what is left out, for example "Triple lock, every year by the rule (no exceptions)" and "Double lock, every year by the rule". Move the first sentence of "About the calculated column" up beside the menu. The menu is in `src/tools/history.njk` line 57, the explanation in `src/assets/js/lib/history-view.js` line 138.
